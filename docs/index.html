Plant Wind Forecast Benchmark

Three time-series foundation models, used zero-shot, and an XGBoost model trained on the plant's own 2014 history forecast a single wind farm, ENGIE's La Haute Borne in France, the way Indian real-time scheduling works: every 4 hours, forecast the next 16 blocks of 15 minutes. Two input setups. A: past power only, where each run sees the actual output up to the moment it is issued, including the 16 blocks measured since the previous run. B: the same past power plus the wind speed over the next 4 hours (ERA5 100 m), standing in for the wind forecast a plant would receive. Errors are shown as a percentage of installed capacity.

With a realistic wind forecast

Setup B again, but the wind input is ERA5 degraded into something closer to an operational forecast. Per run, a timing error of up to ±1 h and a level bias (sd 10%) apply to the whole wind series the model sees, as they would when its past values come from the same forecast product. On top of that, an error drifts over the next 4 hours, starting from zero at the run time. The noise is identical for every model, and XGBoost is trained on equally noisy wind. (A first version degraded only the future part, which created an artificial jump at the run time that t0-alpha reacted to strongly; it was corrected before publishing.) The dots show each model's nMAE for A (past power only), B with noisy wind and B with ERA5.

How each model was tuned

Every model got a validation budget on October–December 2014 (runs every 4 h); the 2015 test year was never used to choose anything. Foundation models chose their history length (512 / 1,024 / 2,048 / 4,096 blocks) and whether to receive time of day, which XGBoost always has, as a known-future input.

    Caveats

    • Setup B's wind speed is ERA5 reanalysis (hourly, 100 m, interpolated to 15 min), which is closer to the truth than a real operational forecast, so B's gains are an optimistic upper bound. ERA5 correlates 0.80 with plant output, versus 0.91 for the turbines' own anemometers. It lines up best with power about 1 hour later; no timing correction was applied.
    • The power-curve baseline maps ERA5 wind speed to power deciles per 0.5 m/s bin, fitted on 2014 only.
    • XGBoost: one multi-quantile model shared across steps, predicting the change from the last observed block (so it is anchored to the latest actual value). Features: 16 power lags, rolling means and spread, time of day, and in B the ERA5 wind over the horizon. Its design was checked on a validation split inside 2014 (train Jan–Sep, validate Oct–Dec), never on the 2015 test year. It is trained on this plant; the foundation models are not. Its ms/run includes training time.
    • The data is from 2014–2015 and ships with NREL's OpenOA library, so it may be in some foundation models' pretraining data (t0-alpha does not disclose its training data). That would flatter the foundation models, not XGBoost. Only an unseen plant, such as your own, rules this out.
    • One plant and one test year, with runs always at 00/04/08/12/16/20 UTC: the ranking may not transfer to other sites or schedules.
    • TimesFM 3.0 weights are licensed for non-commercial use only; it is included here as a research reference, not a deployable option.
    • Gaps in the context (≤10% allowed) were linearly interpolated, and every model received identical inputs. Forecasts were clipped to [0, capacity].
